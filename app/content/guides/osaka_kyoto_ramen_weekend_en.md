Osaka and Kyoto sit less than 30 minutes apart by train, which makes them the easiest weekend ramen pairing in Japan. Osaka leans into bold, late-night, drinking-culture bowls; Kyoto has its own surprisingly heavy "kotteri" tradition alongside one of the country's densest ramen streets. This course splits the two days by city so you're not backtracking on the JR Kyoto Line more than once.

## Before you go

*   **Get an ICOCA or similar IC card.** Osaka–Kyoto trains run constantly on the JR Kyoto Line and Hankyu/Keihan lines; you don't need reserved seats for this trip.
*   **Base yourself in either city.** Staying one night in Osaka and one in Kyoto works well, or you can day-trip from a single base — the ride is about 15–29 minutes depending on the train.
*   **Confirm hours before each stop.** Regional shops, especially smaller Kyoto counters, close on irregular days. Check Google Maps the morning of, since this itinerary is editorial planning, not a live booking source.

## Day 1 — Saturday in Osaka

### Late morning: arrival and a tonkotsu warm-up

If you're coming in via Shin-Osaka Station, start with [Jinrui Mina Menrui](/ramen/jinrui_mina_menrui_en) in Yodogawa, a shoyu-focused shop that's a short ride from the shinkansen platform — a practical first stop before you check into your accommodation.

**Order:** the classic shoyu ramen, tasting the broth before adding condiments.

### Lunch: tonkotsu in the Namba/Chuo area

Head toward Namba for [Zundoya Osaka](/ramen/zundoya_osaka_en) in Chuo Ward, a solid, unfussy tonkotsu bowl in the middle of Osaka's densest tourist and shopping area (Dotonbori is a 10–15 minute walk).

**Order:** the signature tonkotsu ramen. Add kaedama (extra noodles) if you skipped breakfast.

**Afternoon:** use the gap between lunch and dinner to explore Dotonbori, Shinsaibashi, and Kuromon Ichiba Market — Osaka rewards wandering, and you'll want the walking to make room for two more bowls.

### Evening: Umeda's ramen density

Move north to Umeda for [Menya 7.5Hz Umeda](/ramen/menya_7.5hz_umeda_en), a shoyu shop in Kita Ward that fits naturally into an evening around Umeda's bars, department stores, and Sky Building viewpoint.

**Order:** classic shoyu ramen — this is a good bowl to eat slowly since the area invites lingering afterward.

### Late night: last bowl before bed

Still in Kita, close the day with [Ramen Yashichi](/ramen/ramen_yashichi_en), a chicken-broth option that works well as a late finisher after a night out — very much in the Osaka tradition of a ramen bowl capping off the evening.

**Reality check:** three or four bowls is already a full day. If you're traveling with kids or just don't want to eat that much ramen in one day, drop the late-night stop and treat Yashichi as a lunch option on a future trip.

## Day 2 — Sunday in Kyoto

Take the JR Special Rapid Service from Osaka Station to Kyoto Station — about 29 minutes.

### Morning: shoyu right at Kyoto Station

Start with [Honke Daiichi-Asahi](/ramen/honke_daiichi-asahi_en) in Shimogyo, one of the most convenient stops in this whole guide since it sits close to Kyoto Station itself. It's a legendary, no-frills shoyu bowl and a good way to fuel up before a day of temple-hopping.

**Order:** classic shoyu ramen first; add chashu-men if you want extra pork.

### Midday: tonkotsu in Higashiyama

Work your way toward Kiyomizu-dera and the Higashiyama sightseeing district, and stop at [Ramen Miyako](/ramen/ramen_miyako_en) for a tonkotsu bowl tucked into one of Kyoto's most walkable historic neighborhoods.

**Order:** the signature tonkotsu ramen, with extra chashu if you're planning a lot of walking afterward.

### Afternoon: Kyoto Gogyo's kogashi ramen

Head into central Kyoto (Nakagyo/Gion area) for [Kyoto Gogyo](/ramen/kyoto_gogyo_en), known for its distinctive kogashi (burnt/charred garlic oil) shoyu broth — a visually striking, deeply smoky bowl that's genuinely different from the shoyu you had that morning.

**Order:** the shop's kogashi-style shoyu ramen; taste the broth before mixing in the charred oil fully so you can register the contrast.

### Evening: Ichijoji, Kyoto's "Ramen Street"

Finish in Sakyo Ward at [Tenkaippin Main Shop](/ramen/tenkaippin_main_shop_en), the flagship of a chain famous for its extremely thick "kotteri" broth — a Kyoto specialty that surprises visitors expecting the city's food to be delicate. The main shop sits in the Ichijoji neighborhood, informally known as Kyoto's ramen street for its unusually high concentration of quality shops.

**Order:** the kotteri (rich) broth if it's your first visit — it's the shop's defining style.

**If you have a third day or a car:** [Muteppou Kyoto](/ramen/muteppou_kyoto_en) in Kizugawa is a heavier tonkotsu specialist worth the extra trip south of the city for dedicated pork-broth fans, though it's far enough from central Kyoto that it doesn't fit neatly into this two-day frame.
