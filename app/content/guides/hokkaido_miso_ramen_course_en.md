Miso ramen was born in Sapporo, and nowhere else in Japan lets you trace the style from its origin to its modern variations in the space of a weekend. This course spends two days almost entirely within Sapporo's city center, prioritizing the shops that actually shaped the miso tradition over sheer variety. If you have a third day, an optional Asahikawa side trip at the end offers a useful shoyu comparison to put the miso style in context.

## Before you go

*   **Hokkaido is cold for more of the year than the rest of Japan.** Even shoulder-season visits can be brisk, and miso ramen's richness is partly a response to that climate — eat it as intended, hot and immediately.
*   **Susukino is Sapporo's nightlife and ramen district.** Several stops on this course cluster there, so you can walk between some of them rather than relying on the subway.
*   **Confirm hours before each visit.** Sapporo's older, family-run miso shops sometimes close for irregular days or run seasonal hours. This itinerary is editorial planning, not a live booking source — check Google Maps the morning of.

## Day 1 — The origin story

### Morning: the birthplace of miso ramen

Start at [Aji no Sanpei](/ramen/aji_no_sanpei_en) in Chuo Ward, the shop widely credited with inventing miso ramen in the 1950s. It's a genuine pilgrimage stop for anyone serious about ramen history, not just a novelty — the miso tare recipe here is the ancestor of everything else on this list.

**Order:** the house miso ramen as-is on your first visit, before trying butter-corn or spicy miso variations elsewhere. This is the baseline against which every other bowl on this course gets compared.

### Midday: the legendary thick bowl

Move to [Sumire Sapporo](/ramen/sumire_sapporo_en), known for one of the thickest, most intensely flavored miso broths in the city — a noticeably different, heavier style than the birthplace shop's original recipe.

**Order:** house miso ramen. The broth here is strong enough that you may not want to add extra oil or spice on a first visit.

### Afternoon: refined, balanced miso

Continue to [Menya Saimi](/ramen/menya_saimi_en), a shop that built its reputation on a more refined, ginger-forward take on miso ramen, often cited alongside Sumire as one of the two essential modern miso experiences in Sapporo.

**Order:** house miso ramen — the ginger aromatics are the point here, so skip heavy customization.

### Evening: Susukino for a modern bowl

Head into Susukino for [Ramen Kuryu](/ramen/ramen_kuryu_en), a well-regarded miso counter that fits naturally into an evening around Sapporo's densest bar and restaurant district.

**Optional late-night stop:** if you still have room, wander through [Ganso Sapporo Ramen Yokocho](/ramen/ganso_sapporo_ramen_yokocho_en), the original ramen alley in Susukino — a lane of small counters where you can compare a half-bowl or two rather than committing to a full dinner-sized order at every stall.

## Day 2 — Modern Sapporo miso

### Morning: a second-generation classic

Start with [Ramen Mutsumiya](/ramen/ramen_mutsumiya_en), another long-running Sapporo miso institution that's easy to fold into a morning before sightseeing.

**Order:** house miso ramen; butter-corn if you want the classic Sapporo garnish combination.

### Midday: Hokkai Ramen

Continue to [Hokkai Ramen](/ramen/hokkai_ramen_en) for a solid, unpretentious bowl that represents the everyday version of Sapporo miso rather than a flashy specialty shop.

**Order:** the standard miso ramen — this is a good stop for comparing an "ordinary" Sapporo bowl against yesterday's more famous names.

### Afternoon: Ramen Gantetsu

Head to [Ramen Gantetsu](/ramen/ramen_gantetsu_en), a well-known counter that many locals name as a personal favorite even though it's less internationally famous than Sumire or Sanpei.

**Order:** house miso ramen; ask about seasonal or limited variations if you want something outside the classic recipe.

### Evening: Ramen Shingen in Susukino

Close the day back in Susukino at [Ramen Shingen](/ramen/ramen_shingen_en), known locally for its miso ramen and its fried rice — a good pairing if you want to round out the meal with something other than a second bowl of noodles.

**Order:** house miso ramen with a side of fried rice (chahan) to share.

## Optional Day 3: Asahikawa shoyu comparison

If you have time for a half-day trip, take the JR limited express from Sapporo Station to Asahikawa (about 1 hour 25 minutes) for [Asahikawa Ramen Aoba](/ramen/asahikawa_ramen_aoba_en), a shop operating since 1947 and a defining name in Asahikawa's shoyu tradition — famous for the thin oil layer that keeps the broth scalding hot from the first sip to the last. It's not a miso shop, but tasting it back-to-back with two days of Sapporo miso makes Hokkaido's regional ramen differences click into place in a way that reading about them never quite does.
