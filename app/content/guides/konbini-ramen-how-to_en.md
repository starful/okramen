## Pick from the situation, not the brand wall

| You are | Buy | Skip |
|---|---|---|
| First night, 11pm, hotel kettle | One cup + onsen egg | Chilled pack (hard to keep until tomorrow) |
| Same, no kettle | Cup. Store hot-water tap, or ask the desk for a kettle | Microwave-only chilled ramen |
| Daytime, raining, no queue patience | Collab cup or chilled pack | A thin standard cup as a full lunch |
| Airbnb with a pot | Bag noodles or pot-cook chilled | Eating in a cramped aisle |
| Souvenirs for the flight | Cups or bags. Supermarket or Don Quijote | Anything from the fridge |
| You want heat | Packaging that says 辛 or 蒙古タンメン | Random tonkotsu |

Short version: **cup for now, chilled for a real meal, bag/cup to pack, spice only when the name says so.**

## Finding the shelf

Japanese convenience stores (konbini) from 7-Eleven, Lawson, and FamilyMart sit on most corners. Ramen is not in one place.

1. **Ambient noodles** — often opposite onigiri and bread, between drinks and snacks. Cups at eye level, bags lower.
2. **Chilled (チルド)** — same fridge as milk, dessert, salad. Labels include 生麺, ラーメン, 焼きそば. Yakisoba packs sit next to ramen. Soup sachets mean ramen; sauce-only usually means yakisoba or mazesoba.
3. **Toppings** — eggs in the egg fridge or next to bakery as onsen tamago. Green onion in small packs, if they have them. Chashu in the chilled deli.
4. **Hot water and microwave** — by the window or the register. Microwave says レンジ. The hot-water unit has a ledge for a cup.
5. **Eat-in (イートイン)** — some stores have two tables; station kiosks often have none.

On the first visit, only the cup wall matters. Chilled packs can wait until the second stop.

Pay with transit IC (Suica, PASMO, ICOCA), card, PayPay, or cash. IC is fastest for a single cup. Chopsticks and spoons sit in a cup by the register. If you skip them, the hotel room is awkward.

Tiny neighborhood stores have one shelf. Big station stores have two, with regionals and collabs mixed in. **Hunt collabs in a large store. Hunt calories in the nearest store.**

## Four formats

### Cup noodles (カップ麺)

The travel default. You only need boiling water.

Steep time is on the lid: 3分 or 5分. Three minutes usually means thinner noodles; five often means a premium “fresh-style” cup. Less water and less time leaves the noodles raw. Fill to the printed line.

ノンフライ (non-fry) noodles are not flash-fried. Maruchan Seimen cups and Nissin Raoh cups sit here. They smell less like oil and chew closer to a shop noodle. They also cost a step more.

Cheap cups use powder soup and dried green onion. Collabs and premiums add a liquid soup or paste in a separate packet. Nakamoto’s red miso paste is the textbook case. **Skip the packet and the bowl is half a bowl.** Open the lid and find the small bags first.

Japanese cups hold less noodle than a large Korean cup. For dinner, add an egg or onigiri.

Names rotate each season. Everyday: Cup Noodle, Charumera. Texture: Raoh, Seimen. Collab: Moko Tanmen Nakamoto, Nakiryu, regional shops.

### Dried bags (袋麺)

For lodging with a pot. The store hot-water tap will not cook them.

Multi-packs of two are cheaper at a supermarket than at a konbini. Bags are the safest souvenir: light, no melt.

Do not cook noodles in the hotel kettle. The kettle will smell like ramen until you check out. No bowl in the bathroom? Skip bags and buy a cup.

### Chilled fresh noodles (チルド生麺)

The top of the konbini ladder. A plastic pack with noodles, soup, and toppings. Most are a few minutes in the microwave, or a pot.

Taste sits between a cup and a shop. Liquid soup beats powder. Shelf life is short. Keep it cold and eat it that day.

Read the cook icons.

- レンジ only: you need a microwave, in the store or in the room.
- 鍋 only: you need a pot.
- Both: use the microwave.

Some packs boil the noodles in a second step. If the diagram has two stages, guessing the water makes the soup too salty. Do not price-match yakisoba chilled packs with ramen; ramen includes soup and usually costs more.

### Famous-shop collabs

A cup that borrows a shop’s signboard flavor when you will not queue. 7-Eleven runs many exclusives; Lawson and FamilyMart have their own.

“Same as the restaurant” on the wrap is marketing. You get salt and aroma, not the kettle. [Instant vs shop](/guide/instant_vs_shop_en) covers the gap.

Collabs still have a clearer character than generic cups. Nakamoto is spicy. Nakiryu is sesame and peanut. Tonkotsu collabs run fatty and opaque. Limited labels (`期間限定` `季節限定`) last weeks. They may be gone on the next trip.

If the collab already sits a price step above a standard cup, skip extra toppings. It is already a meal ticket.

## Which chain to walk into

You do not need a three-store crawl. One job, one chain.

**7-Eleven** rotates collab cups the fastest. It is the first door if you want “that shop, in a cup.” Many stores put the hot-water tap next to Seven Café. Seven Premium eggs and bento pair easily. Big station stores have two noodle rows and mix in regionals. Tiny stores may have a single cup row. Scan the collab row top to bottom. If you see 名店 or 監修, check the broth type and take it.

**Lawson** stocks texture brands such as Raoh and Seimen. Go here when you want a cup that chews well, not a famous name. MACHI café, croquettes, and winter oden make easy sides. Locals do eat oden broth next to a cup; the salt stacks, so pick shoyu or shio. Lawson-only PB prints LAWSON large on the pack. Default buy: one non-fry cup plus an onsen egg. For tonkotsu, start with a rich Raoh.

**FamilyMart** wins on Famichiki. Ramen plus fried chicken is a meal. Keep the chicken beside the cup, not in the soup. Fewer collabs than 7-Eleven; everyday cups and chilled packs are steady. The salad case is easier to use as a side for a heavy cup. If you skip 7-Eleven and expect a wall of famous-shop cups, FamilyMart will look quiet.

Ministop is known for soft serve; the noodle wall is thin. Station brands such as NewDays often stock commute cups only. For ramen, stay on 7-Eleven, Lawson, and FamilyMart.

If all three sit on the same block: spice and collabs at 7-Eleven, noodle texture at Lawson, hunger plus chicken at FamilyMart.

## What to read on the pack

You do not need every kanji.

| On the pack | Means | When to choose it |
|---|---|---|
| 醤油 shoyu | Soy broth | Safe first cup |
| 味噌 miso | Miso | Hokkaido-ish, heavy |
| 塩 shio | Salt | Light, morning, heat |
| 豚骨 tonkotsu | Pork bone | Rich, Kyushu |
| 担々 / 坦々 tantan | Sesame heat | Not Korean jjajang |
| 辛 / 激辛 | Spicy | Not the same heat as Korean fire noodles. Nakamoto builds as you eat |
| ノンフライ | Non-fried noodles | Texture first |
| 生麺タイプ | Fresh-style | Longer steep even in a cup |
| 熱湯 | Boiling water | Kettle or store tap |
| レンジ seconds/minutes | Microwave | Skip if you have no microwave |
| 名店 / 監修 | Shop-supervised | Collab |
| 期間限定 / 季節限定 | Limited / seasonal | Fine to buy today |
| 地域限定 | Regional | Buy it in that region |
| 2食入 | Two servings | May be leftover if you are solo |
| お湯を注ぐ | Pour hot water | Cup |
| 茹でる | Boil | Pot |

Allergens sit on the back under 原材料: wheat, buckwheat, milk, egg, peanut. Sesame tantan often includes peanut.

A common miss for Korean travelers is hunting spice inside tonkotsu. Most tonkotsu cups are not spicy. Heat is a separate SKU.

## Region and season

Konbini logistics run by regional companies. A Tokyo 7-Eleven and a Sapporo 7-Eleven do not share the same noodle bay.

Hokkaido leans miso, butter corn, seafood, especially in winter. Kyushu leans tonkotsu and Hakata shop collabs. Okinawa soba cups can sit in the ramen row. The noodles are thick and the soup is dashi. It is not ramen. Buy it if you want, but change the expectation. Tokyo and Kanto lean shoyu, dark soy, tantan, Nakamoto-style heat.

Summer brings hiyashi (chilled) packs. If you only scan hot cups you miss half the bay. Autumn adds mushroom and miso seasonals. Winter pairs miso, butter, and oden next door. 期間限定 is a reason to buy on this trip even when the name is not on a top-five list. Check broth type and cook method, then take it.

## How to cook it there

Most stores have a hot-water tap and a microwave.

Pay first, then fill the cup to the line. Close the lid and wait the printed minutes. Chopsticks are by the counter. If you need more water, `お湯をお願いします` is enough.

Some stores have eat-in seats; some do not. Do not eat standing in front of the fridge. No seat: take it to the hotel. Do not dump broth in the sink. Close the cup lid and use the trash. Shop-bowl broth manners live in [this guide](/guide/ramen_noodle_disposal_manners_en). The place is different.

If the hotel has a kettle, cups are easier in the room, especially at a busy station 7-Eleven. If the kettle has not actually boiled, noodles stay raw. Wait for steam.

For microwave packs, crack the lid and follow the film diagram. Times run longer than a cup. At a busy lunch microwave, a six-minute chilled pack holds up the bento line. Switch to a cup.

## Building a meal with toppings

One cup is a snack for a travel dinner. Add from the same ticket.

Onsen tamago first on spicy miso and tantan. Small green onion packs on shoyu and shio. Nori and menma on shoyu if you see them. Chashu slices with chilled ramen. One onigiri can go into leftover broth. You do not need a pretty bowl. Famichiki and karaage sit beside the cup. Dropped in the soup they over-salt it.

Loose pairings:

- Shoyu: egg, nori, green onion. A highball is lighter than beer.
- Tonkotsu: egg, chashu. Green onion cuts the fat.
- Miso: butter in Hokkaido. You do not have to force it onto a Tokyo cup.
- Tantan and Nakamoto: an egg is close to required. Some people grab milk.
- Shio: too many toppings muddy the soup. Onion only.

A convenience-store highball belongs to late night. Salt and alcohol before a day of sightseeing make the next shop bowl taste flat. A proper after-drinking bowl is in the [shime guide](/guide/late_night_culture_en). A konbini cup is shime when the shop has closed.

## Price bands versus a shop bowl

Exact yen prices move by store and tax display. Use bands for trip math, not a receipt.

A standard cup is roughly a quarter to a fifth of a shop bowl. Collab and premium cups sit above that. One chilled meal sits between a cup and a shop. Eggs, chashu, and onion can lift a cup meal more than you expect. Shop bowls, in the [price guide](/guide/ramen_prices_en), usually cost several cups.

Konbini ramen is not “cheap ramen.” It is ramen that does not spend money on a queue or opening hours. A collab cup plus egg can lose to a shop at lunch. Konbini wins at midnight, in rain, solo, and the night before a flight.

## When the purchase is a bad fit

Microwave-only, no microwave in the room. Chilled noodles in an afternoon suitcase: they thaw. Hunting Korean-style heat in a random cup: spice is printed (Nakamoto and 辛). You do not have to skip every pack missing from a top-five list. Seasonals are the point of this trip. Confirm broth and cook method.

A heavy tonkotsu cup before hotel breakfast makes the morning slow. Morning: shio or shoyu. Building a souvenir box at a konbini means a thin, expensive set. Use the [instant souvenir guide](/guide/best-instant-ramen-japan_en) and go to a supermarket or Don Quijote.
